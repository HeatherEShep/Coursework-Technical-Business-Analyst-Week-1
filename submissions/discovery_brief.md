## 1. Problem summary

Context & Legacy Problem Statement

While Legacy Trust Bank has spent years modernising its primary customer-facing banking journeys, debt recovery did not receive the same attention. As the bank expanded across personal loans, credit cards, and auto finance, its collections environment never evolved into a unified system. Today, more than 50 representatives rely on a fragile network of spreadsheets, email trails, and a 20-year-old legacy collections database to manage over 100,000 delinquent accounts. Initial local workarounds, such as localised tracking sheets and manual email templates, helped in the short term. However, they have left the bank with an operating model held together by individual memory, redundant effort, and manual data reconciliation.

Operational Failure at Scale

As delinquent account volumes rose, these manual adaptations stopped coping. Static spreadsheets and disconnected communication channels create data latency and zero real-time visibility for managers, making it impossible to verify which follow-ups have actually occurred. Representatives update statuses differently depending on their own habits. Various issues have emerged as a result. Straightforward balance confirmations and high-complexity cases are mixed together in the same manual queue. Representatives spend large portions of their day checking whether a colleague has already contacted a customer, reconciling conflicting files, and manually tracking promises to pay, meaning that time is diverted away from accounts that genuinely require human judgment. Customers are pushed through slow, repetitive, and inconsistent contact cycles, leading to missed follow-ups, duplicated outreach, and eroded trust.

Financial Impact & Value Case Scrutiny

Inconsistent follow-through and delayed outreach are contributing to an estimated 15% revenue loss through preventable roll-through and missed recovery opportunities. However, leadership will not approve a solution based on enthusiasm or top-line projections alone. To build a credible case, the financial model must withstand rigorous scrutiny by: isolating hard operational cost savings (FTE capacity reclaimed from manual tasks) from speculative recovery uplift, demonstrating transparent assumptions backed by sensible sensitivity testing and proving that proposed interventions can deliver a full payback within 12 months. Leadership hypothesises that a customer self-serve portal, which would allow customers to confirm balances, choose next steps, and resolve straightforward cases independently, will reduce friction and reclaim capacity. However, discovery must evaluate this hypothesis objectively alongside other potential automation opportunities rather than treating it as a pre-determined outcome.

Phase 1 Decision Gates & Evidence Requirements

To secure Phase 1 approval, leadership requires a clear, evidence-based picture of where the current collections process is breaking down, how those breakdowns translate into wasted effort and lost recoveries, and which automation opportunities genuinely add value. This requires an honest end-to-end process map of the current workflow exposing where missed follow-ups, duplicated effort, and handling-time inflation occur. Crucially, this map must clearly define where automated self-service ends and human representative intervention begins, ensuring messy hand-offs do not default back onto frontline teams. Leadership also requires an objective ranking of automation opportunities based on 12-month ROI, implementation effort, and net P&L impact, supported by a clear line of sight linking every identified process breakdown to a validated user need, a future-state workflow, and buildable product requirements.

Stakeholder Alignment & Core Challenge

The primary challenge of Phase 1 is bridging the natural tension between Operations (demanding a realistic workflow their teams can adopt), Finance & Compliance (demanding defensible, evidence-based savings and regulatory alignment), and Product (demanding clear, traceable requirements to shape a backlog). Phase 1 will succeed only if it delivers a strategy that is operationally honest, commercially credible, and execution-ready.

## 3. Discovery questions

- What steps in the workflow show the highest levels of delay, rework, or inconsistency?
- Which parts of the process rely on memory, personal habits or undocumented rules?
- Where does information get lost, duplicated, or require manual reconciliation?
- What information do customers routinely request that could be retrieved automatically?
- Which specific account criteria should automatically disqualify an account from self-service eligibility? 
- Which parts of the workflow create the most confusion or disagreements between representatives and managers? 
- Which metrics would leadership consider essential for proving the need for improvement, and highlight operational efficiency?

Problem statement: 

Legacy Trust's collections process can not keep up with rising delinquent volumes. Inconsistent updates and manual workarounds are causing missed follow ups, duplicated effort, and avoidable delays that drain capacity and drive loss. Without a unified workflow or meaningful automation, representatives spend more time checking and reconciling than collecting, putting both operational performance and financial outcomes at risk.
