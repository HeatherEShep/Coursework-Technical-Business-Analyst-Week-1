## Jobs to be done

1. **Customer — clarity and choice:** When I need to resolve an outstanding balance, I want to see exactly what I owe and the options available, so I can choose a manageable way to pay.  
   Inspired by [Dr Lynda Smith’s observation](#evidence-customer-balance-options): “Customers would pay more readily if they understood exactly what they owe and could see options.”

2. **Customer — continuity across channels:** When my case moves between departments, I want my history and previous conversations to follow me, so I do not have to explain my situation again.  
   Inspired by [Robert Quinn’s observation](#evidence-customer-repeat-story): “Customers get transferred between departments and have to repeat their entire situation each time.”

3. **Collections representative — reliable contact history:** When I pick up a case, I want to see recent contacts and promised callbacks in one reliable place, so I can follow through without duplicating outreach.  
   Inspired by [Christopher Richards’s observation](#evidence-representative-contact-history): “The collections database does not sync with the email tracker, so representatives often re-contact customers who were already promised callbacks.”

4. **Operations manager — effective case routing:** When new cases enter the workflow, I want them routed according to their needs and complexity, so straightforward cases are not delayed behind cases requiring specialist handling.  
   Inspired by [Priya Nair’s observation](#evidence-operations-queue): “Simple cases that could be resolved in minutes take days because they get stuck in the wrong queue.”

5. **Customer — accessible account history:** When I need an update on my account, I want to see a clear record of past conversations, actions, and next steps, so I can check my progress without calling for information I have already been given.  
   Inspired by [Diana White’s observation](#evidence-customer-visible-history): “We are exposed to complaint risk because customers cannot track what we have told them.” The repeated calls in [Daniel Okoye’s observation](#evidence-customer-repeat-calls) also show the impact when customers cannot easily revisit that information.

6. **Finance analyst — trusted forecasting data:** When forecasting recovery revenue, I want timely, reliable activity data, so I can produce forecasts with confidence.  
   Inspired by [Daniel Farmer’s observation](#evidence-finance-forecasting): “The finance team cannot forecast recovery revenue because they do not trust the activity data.”

7. **Operations manager — traceable case history:** When reviewing a case, I want its audit trail available in one place, so I can demonstrate what happened without reconciling scattered records.  
   Inspired by [Andrea Hunt’s observation](#evidence-compliance-audit-trail): “The audit trail is scattered across email, spreadsheets, and the legacy database, making compliance reviews a nightmare.”

## Top 3 JTBD

JTBD-01	Customer When I need to resolve an outstanding balance, I want to see exactly what I owe and the options available, so I can choose a manageable way to pay.

Prioritising JTBD-01 addresses an immediate operational bottleneck: over 100,000 accounts currently sit in manual, overburdened queues, where customers are left with a lack of clarity and transparency. This initiative is directly backed by cross-functional evidence, notably Compliance Liaison Tracy Field believes that "the portal could work brilliantly for the 40% of customers who want to resolve this themselves", reinforced by Operations Analyst Dr Lynda Smith’s observation that "customers would pay more readily if they understood exactly what they owe and could see options", and Head of Debt Recovery Operations Amina Rahman's finding that debtors respond best to control and transparency. Phase 1 could focus on letting customers see what they owe, and set up simple payment plans online, which quickly takes care of the 40% of customers who want to resolve this themselves. 

JTBD-04	Operations manager	When new cases enter the workflow, I want them routed according to their needs and complexity, so straightforward cases are not delayed behind cases requiring specialist handling.

Prioritising JTBD-04 eliminates a major operational bottleneck where simple, quick accounts are trapped in the same queue as complex cases, bringing daily progress to a standstill. This is directly supported by Product Manager Priya Nair, who notes that "simple cases that could be resolved in minutes take days because they get stuck in the wrong queue", as well as Operations Analyst Barry Skinner’s observation that routine cases are routinely delayed behind complex ones with no priority logic. In Phase 1, introducing basic complexity sorting acts as the central traffic controller, funnelling straightforward accounts directly into the online self-service portal while helping to clear queues so representatives can focus on cases that genuinely require human time and discretion.

JTBD-10	Customer service representative	When a customer may be experiencing hardship or vulnerability, I want to assess their circumstances with appropriate discretion, so they receive support that fits their situation rather than an unsuitable automated decision.

JTBD-10 addresses the problem of identifying customers that need further attention since they could be vulnerable.

Prioritising JTBD-10 establishes the critical ethical and regulatory safety shield for the entire project: automating collections without a way to protect vulnerable customers creates massive compliance risks and severe regulatory penalties. Customer Service Lead Peter Chambers highlights the core challenge: "We cannot automate the decision about whether a customer is genuinely in hardship or just dodging contact". This is further highlighted by Operations Analyst Stewart Howell’s finding that "cases involving vulnerable customers are not flagged differently from routine cases", and Service Design Lead Eleanor Clark’s warning that "we have edge cases involving hardship, vulnerability, and regulatory forbearance that need human judgment". For Phase 1 scope, JTBD-10 serves as an essential off-ramp: the portal must never make automated decisions on vulnerable accounts, but instead immediately freeze collections activity and route the customer directly to a compassionate, trained specialist.
